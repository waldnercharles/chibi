# Scaling an interacting material world

Historical assessment from October 2, 2026, before the MPM implementation. See the
[material showcase guide](material-showcase.md) for the implemented solvers,
gravity, buoyancy, body torque, deformation, and current measurements.

Assessment, 2026-10-02. The current architecture uses DDR liquids, an optional PIC-like gas velocity grid, terrain masks/Box2D, and fluid thermal exchange. Code inspection and retained measurements establish current implementation behavior; papers below establish algorithm properties, not Chibi speedups.

## Recommendation

Retain chunked terrain, masks, rigid fragments, and implemented fluid heat exchange. Extend material identity, properties and reactions across these representations; that common model is not yet implemented. Keep DDR as the measured liquid baseline and the optional gas grid as an approximation with an explicit quality target. First make work scale with **active material and changed boundaries**, rather than total world size. Fine motion needs sufficient spatial resolution and good presentation, irrespective of solver name.

For the Tutti-like visual target, the strongest next comparison is **sparse tiled CPU MLS-MPM with APIC transfers**, initially restricted to dense moving liquids, with terrain/rigid-body boundaries and the same thermal accounting. Include CPIC only when thin separators, cutting, or two-way coupling expose a concrete gap. PBF is a narrower alternative when DDR compressibility or stability is the main failure. Incompressible APIC/FLIP becomes attractive if pressure-driven volume conservation matters more than a general mixed-material continuum. None has a demonstrated frame-budget advantage here yet.

## What each approach buys and costs

| Approach | Quality/use | Work and limits relevant to this game |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cellular material world | Local reactions, excavation, settled material, cheap sleeping regions | Excellent world ownership model; discrete movement rules do not reproduce free-floating blobs, sheets and vortices automatically. Retain continuous particles for those motions. |
| DDR | Coherent cohesive liquids, viscosity, approximate volume conservation | Neighbor search plus repeated local density/displacement work. Resolution, occupancy and substeps matter; it is not an incompressible pressure solve. |
| SPH / PBF | SPH estimates continuum fields from neighbors; PBF iteratively constrains density with a tunable work budget | Stiff weakly-compressible SPH can demand small timesteps. PBF is robust at larger steps, but iterations, boundary density errors, damping and finite propagation of corrections remain costs. |
| PIC / FLIP / APIC liquids | Grid mechanics with particle transport; APIC retains local velocity variation; FLIP reduces PIC dissipation | Transfer choice alone does not enforce incompressibility: pressure projection, free surfaces, boundaries and grid resolution must also be supplied. Sparse grids help empty space, not dense active cells. |
| MPM / MLS-MPM / CPIC | Large deformation, liquids/grains/soft solids; CPIC separates incompatible transfers around discontinuities | Grid transfers replace much neighbor interaction, but constitutive stress, matrix state, contact, active-grid work and stability-driven substeps remain. Cutting support is not an authored brittle-fracture criterion. |
| Coarse gas grid | Shared plume flow and broad coherent motion | Useful visual approximation; PIC averaging dissipates motion, coarse cells miss narrow passages, and a velocity grid without pressure projection is not an incompressible gas solver. |

**Cellular evidence.** Noita developer Petri Purho describes 64×64 chunks with dirty rectangles, four checker-pattern update phases, moving pixels, and rigid bodies whose shapes are recalculated when pixels are destroyed. Most static stone does not routinely collapse; sufficiently large explosions convert selected candidates to collapsing material. The lesson is selective activity, local rules, and multiple motion representations—not a universally simulated continuum. These are the developer's 2019 design account, not a guarantee of the current game's exact code. [Direct developer interview](https://80.lv/articles/noita-a-game-based-on-falling-sand-simulation)

**Particle evidence.** Clavet et al. describe DDR as approximate volume conservation and anti-clustering; spatial hashing finds local neighbors, and additional timesteps improve fast motion and conservation. Macklin/Müller's PBF constrains density through iterative position corrections, explicitly trades incompressibility for fixed iteration cost, and notes damping, solid-boundary stacking and slow propagation across larger particle populations. Their performance examples use CUDA/GTX 680; those numbers do not transfer to CPU/WebAssembly. The PBF paper also distinguishes the timestep-dependent Clavet projection from its own stability formulation. [DDR paper](https://diglib.eg.org/bitstreams/fbe12062-dca1-427c-ba8a-ec78222cec40/download), [PBF paper](https://mmacklin.com/pbf_sig_preprint.pdf), [original interactive SPH paper](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/sca03.pdf)

**Grid-transfer evidence.** The APIC paper identifies PIC's dissipation and FLIP's noise/instability, adding a locally affine particle velocity representation that preserves angular momentum across transfers. That supports testing better rotational motion; it is not a claim that APIC is the cheapest solver. Bridson's course notes separate advection from pressure projection and discuss smoke temperature/concentration transport and solid boundaries. A coarse velocity average alone lacks those guarantees. [APIC paper](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~jteran/papers/JSSTS15.pdf), [Bridson notes](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~rbridson/fluidsimulation/fluids_notes.pdf)

**MPM evidence.** Hu et al.'s MLS-MPM derives optimized stress divergence and CPIC compatibility through colored distance fields for cutting/open boundaries/two-way rigid coupling. Its reported speedup is relative to their prior MPM discretization, not DDR, PBF or Chibi. Their released solver exposes separate simulation/frame timesteps, thread count, material-specific constitutive parameters and water bulk modulus; the small example repeatedly advances simulation before drawing a frame. Thus “one update per frame” cannot be assumed. Actual explicit stability limits depend on discretization, spacing, velocity and material stiffness; they require measurement and a specified timestep policy. [Authors' project](https://yzhu.io/publication/mpmmls2018siggraph/), [released implementation](https://github.com/yuanming-hu/taichi_mpm)

**Tutti evidence.** Its served runtime identifies an MPM interface, CPU pthread WebAssembly and WebGL2 rendering; it requests 300,000 particles for its initial N-body scene. Visible particles are interpolated deformed instanced quads, with small authored sizes; adaptive tile metrics and merge/split/cheap-gas controls appear. This establishes feasibility of a CPU browser path and separates presentation from physics. It does not reveal the exact native equations, APIC/MLS variant, substeps, hard capacity or equal-device performance. See [deployment evidence](tutti-fluid-reference.md) and [live runtime](https://tsp.grantkot.com/assets/singleton-CC0WmPkn.js).

## Current implementation constraints

The current liquid solver uses hardcoded DDR pressure `600*(density-6)` and near-pressure `1200*near_density`, with two halfsteps. It has no deformation-gradient particle state. The active hash limits neighbor work, but tick and raster preparation still scan the entire retained particle population; spatially inactive particles therefore do not make all costs disappear. These are specific reasons to profile storage/visibility scheduling before inferring a solver bottleneck. [Fluid implementation](../../src/runtime/material_fluid.c), [raster preparation](../../src/render/fluid_raster.c)

Thermal visibility queries iterate the obstacle list. Liquid relaxation has no wall-occlusion test before later collision correction, so particles within one kernel across thin walls can influence each other. Unlike gas/liquid kinds do exchange momentum through approximate repulsion and viscosity. Destruction splits masks without a structural stress model; pieces touching a terrain tile edge remain anchored. These affect quality and dense-world scaling independently of DDR versus MPM. Terrain uses 128×128 occupancy chunks, temporary-file streaming rather than durable saves, and synchronous required-tile loading before budgeted prefetch. Preserve chunked ownership; neither that ownership nor Noita's example requires cellular movement for this continuous zero-G target. [Fluid/contact](../../src/runtime/material_fluid.c), [mask fracture](../../src/runtime/material_mask.c), [terrain dimensions](../../src/runtime/material_terrain.h), [streaming](../../src/runtime/material_stream.c)

Fluid/body exchange currently applies linear impulses at body centers, without fluid torque; dynamic Box2D bodies have fixed rotation. The world has zero gravity, and the particle/gas integration has no gravity or thermal buoyancy force. Solids block particle conduction but do not carry a thermal field. Combustion supplies authored heat, consumes fuel and converts spent parcels to smoke; phase changes, material-specific chemistry and deformable-solid constitutive state remain absent. [Body integration](../../src/runtime/scenes/physics.c), [fluid state and reactions](../../src/runtime/material_fluid.c), [gas integration](../../src/runtime/material_flow.c)

## What the 4,024-particle demonstration cannot establish

The reported 7.408 ms is for the 4,024-parcel fixture at 1280×720 on M4 Pro, with half-resolution HRC and one trace level. It includes simulation and scene rendering/GI with a GPU fence, excluding frame pacing; final-screen blit/present was not separately fenced. The headless CPU frame measured 2.977 ms median. These are medians of three runs' percentiles. A successful timing for one small authored scene establishes that workload on that build/device. It does not establish a maximum sustainable population, linear scaling, solver superiority, browser/native parity, whole-world persistence cost, dense neighbor worst cases, or explosion/fracture tail latency. Sleeping particles, spatial occupancy, kernel-to-spacing ratio, iterations/substeps, collider count and changed terrain area can all change the cost at equal particle count. [Retained measurements](material-detail-frames/material-detail.json), [measurement implementation](../../src/automation/scene_preview.c)

In 2D, halving spacing at fixed filled area requires roughly four times the particles. Holding kernel radius fixed also increases neighbors per particle; reducing kernel with spacing preserves approximate neighbor count but can increase the substeps required for fast motion. Reduce render footprint, spacing and support together, preserve mass/energy per area, and judge at the gameplay camera. Small sprites alone cannot add physical detail.

A useful comparison holds material volume, visible detail, boundaries, gravity, thermal/reaction work and frame duration fixed. Exercise isolated zero-G splash, dense pool compression, thin-wall breach, hot/cold mixing, many moving fragments and repeated excavation; include mostly sleeping and widespread active cases. Report density/volume drift, leakage, motion detail, thermal conservation, peak/p95 frame time, active cells, neighbor counts and actual substeps, including snapshot upload and HRC/rendering. Compare native CPU and shipped WebAssembly configurations separately; GPU acceleration is an option, not a prerequisite or an assumed win.

Everything interacting should mean shared ownership and defined local exchange contracts—not all materials needing one motion solver or every particle interacting with every other particle. Conservation, contact across changing terrain, state transfer at phase changes, and stable scheduling are independent design obligations that a solver replacement does not remove.
