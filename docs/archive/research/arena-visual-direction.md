Reviewed September 8, 2026. Target: a nearly top-down **3D Geometry Wars** arena. Read the playfield as a plane, while lighting, visible side faces and effects establish depth. This is a development direction and a content increment, not a claim of reference-level art quality.

## Authored style contract

- **Composition:** almost overhead, with enough tilt to reveal short side faces. The root implementation targets 10 degrees from overhead and a 35-degree field of view. The combat plane should fill the standalone view with a little perimeter breathing room. Editor chrome is assessed separately.
- **Brightness:** charcoal floor first; subdued blue floor marks next; readable cyan player, rose enemies and bright icy shots above those; brief action effects eventually provide the strongest peaks. Persistent decoration must not hide incoming threats.
- **Geometry:** low raised pieces on a plane. Player width/depth 28×20 and height 10, enemy 24×24 and height 14, projectiles 15×5 and height 4. Existing gameplay colliders and motion are unchanged. The floor remains 640×360.
- **Perimeter:** cyan outer rails three units thick and a finer blue inset rail. Both stay inside the existing floor footprint. Low rail elevation prevents a near wall from hiding combat.
- **Surface detail:** the floor is an explicitly unlit charcoal surface to maintain dark negative space; actors retain lit surfaces. World-aligned patterns establish movement through space; object-aligned detail establishes actor orientation. The floor uses a quiet diamond grid and charcoal variation. Avoid tiny high-contrast noise that turns into shimmer at normal play scale.
- **Palette:** cyan/ice for the player and shots, warm rose for the current pursuer, blue/teal for the arena, charcoal for empty space. These roles can expand with enemy and pickup design; do not recolor the same silhouette indiscriminately.
- **Material authoring:** expose recognizable Mapping, Gradient, Color Ramp and Fresnel operations where they express the effect directly. Keep repeated calculations in one named function; do not replace long arithmetic roots with equally opaque giant functions.

## Current increment and its limits

Authored changes lower the raised bodies and perimeter, split the boundary into cyan and blue rails, darken the floor, and strengthen player/enemy hue separation. Four inset rails are ordinary scene instances with material overrides. They are visual geometry and have no collider or behavior.

The procedural floor and actor material pass now uses Mapping, Gradient, Color Ramp, Fractal Noise and Fresnel nodes. Player and enemy roots have 13 and 9 nodes; the floor has 10 root nodes plus its existing local grid function; the projectile surface has 4 nodes. The former manual Edge Glow library function was removed after both consumers moved to Fresnel. The player receives a forward color gradient in object space. **A brighter nose is only an orientation cue. It does not turn an upright cone into a directional solid.** The first standalone capture confirmed that limitation, prompting the engine increment to add a vertically extruded Triangle Prism with a triangular XZ footprint pointing along local +X. ArenaPlayer uses that actual directional geometry while retaining its authored dimensions and independent collider. Broader composed and hollow bodies remain a geometry-authoring gap. The typed ramp stops use general nested-struct/array serializer support. The standalone capture `/tmp/arena-game-topdown.png` confirmed the new camera composition, rotated floor grid, layered rails, colored bodies and the player’s directional color cue. It also exposed cross-shaped rail corners, corrected by making the horizontal and vertical spans meet flush, and an overly gray lit floor, corrected with the explicit unlit charcoal surface. The refreshed capture shows the actual triangular prism and its visible side faces. Native rendering, picking, editor interaction and save/reopen checks pass. That earlier capture predates the HDR pass. The current `/tmp/arena-hdr-first.png` capture shows restrained halos around the player, enemies and rails while the floor remains dark. Contact depth remains missing. To inspect the authoring workflow, open ArenaPlayer and change a Color Ramp stop or Mapping rotation; open ArenaFloor and change Tile Size or Grid Glow; save and reopen both.

No encounter counts, spawn rates, weapon rates, movement speeds, health, collision bounds or performance acceptance thresholds were changed for this visual pass. Capture ordinary played frames after the authored encounter has spawned enemies, including moving and firing. Do not add inert enemies or alter the simulation to manufacture a crowded screenshot.

## Strongest missing rendering capabilities

1. **Action-driven light and glow.** HDR emission and controlled bloom now supply a stable bright core and local halo, with scene-authored exposure, strength, threshold and radius. The quiet standalone frame preserves dark floor space. The remaining work is to connect light peaks to impacts and other actions, then prove player visibility in crowded moving combat with normal and reduced glow.
2. **Composed and hollow solids.** Triangle Prism supplies a real forward-pointing footprint, but the primitive palette still cannot express hollow prisms, gears or composed enemy bodies. Add reusable geometry/solid authoring with matching picking, bounds and previews; keep actor construction authored rather than adding a renderer special case for each enemy.
3. **Contact depth and coherent effects.** Shadows or a bounded contact-occlusion treatment would anchor raised pieces. Authored ribbons, short impact lines, fragments and expanding indicators would make actions read over time. Their lifetimes, orientation and intensity need effect controls rather than more decorative background nodes.

These are quality gaps, not claims that those exact techniques power the references. A successful next comparison needs a standalone play capture at normal scale, a quiet frame, a crowded moving/firing frame, and a player-visible frame during the strongest effect. Verify both normal and reduced glow before calling the result a showcase.
